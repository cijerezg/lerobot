# Which part of the fine-tuned model carries the robot skill

Written 2026-11-17. Covers the experiments of 2026-11-15 to 2026-11-17.

## Short version

The model has three parts: a vision encoder, a language model, and an action expert.
We took our fine-tuned checkpoint and put the released (base) weights back into one part at a time.

1. Putting the base vision encoder back changes almost nothing. Actions are the same, sim success is the same.
2. Putting the base language model back breaks everything. In sim the arm never reaches an object.
3. The base action expert, never trained by us, already follows our step texts when it sits on our language model.
4. So the robot skill sits mostly in the language model. Not in the vision encoder, and less in the action expert than we assumed.
5. Fine-tuning did change the vision encoder. The change cost it image question answering and bought nothing we can measure.
6. The arm follows the named object only weakly. It goes mostly by position.

## The models

| name | what it is |
|---|---|
| base | released MolmoAct2, `outputs/models/MolmoAct2` |
| internal | our run `rebot-internal-only-20261021`, checkpoint 1200 |
| tube | our run `rebot-v2-tube-b128-20261114`, checkpoint 1200 (another task, another prompt format) |

All three have the same weight names and shapes, so a part of one can be loaded into another.

## 1. What fine-tuning does to chat ability

We asked the checkpoints plain questions, with and without an image (`adhocs/text_probe`).

| | base | tube 1200 | internal 1200 |
|---|---|---|---|
| text facts right (of 35) | 31-35 | 31-35 | 31-35 |
| one-word answers about 20 photos right | 19 | 9 | 11 |

- Text knowledge survives fine-tuning.
- Image answers get worse with every training step (tube: 19, 18, 14, 12, 9 at steps 0, 100, 400, 700, 1200).
- Internal answers most prompts in action tokens. When action tokens are forbidden, the text answers are right. The knowledge is still there.
- With the base vision encoder under a fine-tuned language model, image answers come back: 82 of 82 yes/no and 18-19 of 20 one-word.

So the loss of image answers is in the vision encoder. The language model is fine.
The vision encoder moves early: the cosine between its output tokens and base's is 0.66 after 100 steps and 0.48 after 1200 (tube run).

## 2. Actions on recorded frames (backbone crossing)

Internal's action expert stays. The vision encoder is base or internal. The language model is base, tube or internal.
160 recorded frames, 4 noise seeds, the same noise in every cell. Numbers are fingertip distances in cm.

| vision encoder | language model | distance to the recorded actions | distance to all-internal, same seed |
|---|---|---|---|
| internal | internal | 3.21 | 0 |
| base | internal | 3.26 | 1.62 |
| internal | base | 6.27 | 5.60 |
| base | base | 5.91 | 5.40 |
| internal | tube | 5.81 | 5.33 |
| base | tube | 5.74 | 5.33 |

For scale: two noise seeds of all-internal differ by 2.33 cm.

- Base vision encoder with internal's language model: 1.62 cm from all-internal. That is less than the difference between two seeds.
- Any other language model: about 5.5 cm away, and almost twice as far from the recorded actions.
- With a foreign language model it no longer matters which vision encoder is used.

## 3. Following the step text (step swap)

On the same frames we replaced the step text with "return to home" and measured how far the action moves toward the home pose.

| model | moves toward home (cm) | frames that move toward home |
|---|---|---|
| all internal | 5.43 | 144 of 157 |
| internal vision + language model, base action expert | 4.47 | 136 of 157 |
| base vision + language model, internal action expert | 0.15 | 94 of 157 |

- The base action expert was never trained by us. On internal's language model it gets about 80% of the move.
- Internal's action expert on the base language model gets none of it.

Following the step text comes with the language model, not with the action expert.

## 4. Sim: two objects, one named (object choice)

Scenes with a basket and two objects: the pill bottle plus the orange bottle (both in the training data), a banana, or a mug (never seen).
The instruction names one object. Each layout exists twice, with the two objects exchanged on the same spots.
Depth is off in every cell. 144 episodes per cell.

| cell | went to the named object | went to the other | went to neither | success |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| all internal | 91 | 40 | 13 | 30 |
| base vision encoder, rest internal | 109 | 27 | 8 | 33 |
| base vision encoder + base language model, internal action expert | 0 | 0 | 144 | 0 |

Does the arm follow the object when the two are exchanged? There are 72 such pairs of episodes per cell.

| cell | named object both times | same spot both times |
|---|---|---|
| all internal | 21 | 40 |
| base vision encoder, rest internal | 37 | 27 |

- The closed-loop result agrees with sections 2 and 3. The base vision encoder is at least as good. The base language model gives nothing.
- All-internal goes mostly to a position. This holds even for the two trained bottles (named both times 3 of 24, same spot 18 of 24).
- With the base vision encoder the arm follows the noun more often, also for the banana and the mug (27 of 48).
- When both objects are on the robot's left, both working cells go to the named one about half the time (25 and 29 of 48).

We also asked each cell where the objects are, with action tokens forbidden.

| question | right answers, every cell |
|---|---|
| Which side of the image is the object on? | 71 or 72 of 72 |
| Which side is it on from the robot's point of view? (the camera faces the robot, so the opposite side) | 0 to 2 of 36 |

Every cell finds every object in the image, the banana and the mug included. None turns the view around, the released model included.

## What this adds up to

- **The language model is where the task is learned.** Its fine-tuned weights are needed for the actions, for the step text, and for any success in sim.
- **The vision encoder's training is not needed.** It drifts a lot, the drift costs image answers, and the base encoder does the robot task equally well. The language model accepts either encoder.
- **The action expert is the least specific part.** The base expert already reads the step from our language model. Our expert cannot read anything from the base language model.
- **The model's knowledge reaches the actions only weakly.** Each cell can say where the banana is. The arm still goes mostly by position. With the base vision encoder it does better, which fits section 1: the fine-tuned encoder lost some of what tells objects apart.

## Limits

- Sim: one run, 4 episodes per layout. The 109 against 91 and the 37 against 21 need a repeat before they are stated as a gain. Videos not reviewed.
- Internal was trained with depth tokens. The sim cells ran without them.
- Sections 2 and 3 are single predictions on recorded frames, not closed loop.
- Internal's action expert was trained together with internal's language model. A base language model fails with it for that reason alone. This does not show that the base language model lacks the information.
- One checkpoint of one run. The vision drift numbers come from the tube run.
- The home pose in section 3 is a rounded guess.

## Open

A training run with the vision encoder frozen. The results say it should cost nothing on the task, keep image answers, and may follow the named object better.
